Treatment

Root canal treatment

A root canal treats infection inside the tooth, so an aching or abscessed tooth can be kept rather than removed. The inflamed pulp is cleaned out, disinfected and sealed.

Typically 1–2 visitsLocal anaestheticSaves the toothAbout 60 minutes

Benefits

Why patients choose it

  • Pain that lingers long after something hot or cold
  • A tooth that hurts when you bite, or wakes you at night
  • Swelling, or a small gum boil near the tooth
  • A cracked or deeply decayed tooth that an X-ray shows is still saveable
Procedure overview

What happens, step by step

  1. The tooth is numbed, so the appointment itself is painless.
  2. The infected tissue is removed and the canals cleaned, disinfected and shaped.
  3. The tooth is sealed, and a crown is usually planned to protect it afterwards.
Duration

How long it takes

Booked as a 60-minute appointment. Typically 1–2 visits in practice; the exact plan is confirmed after the examination.

Afterwards

What to expect later

Mild tenderness for a day or two is normal. Avoid chewing hard food on that tooth until the final restoration is in place.

FAQ

Questions patients ask

Is a root canal painful?
No. The tooth and the surrounding gum are fully numbed before anything starts, so the appointment itself is comfortable. Most patients say it feels similar to having a filling done.
How many visits does it take?
Usually one to two visits depending on how many canals the tooth has and how infected it is. You will be told at the first visit which of the two applies to you.
Will the tooth need a crown?
Back teeth almost always do, because the tooth becomes brittle once the pulp is removed. Front teeth sometimes only need a filling. The crown is planned and priced with the root canal, not sprung on you later.
What does it cost?
It depends on the tooth, the number of canals and whether a crown is needed. The clinic examines first, then quotes the whole plan before starting. Nothing begins without you agreeing to the number.
